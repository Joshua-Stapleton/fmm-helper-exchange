# Why this finite census covers every fully canonical basis

Let M be the fixed 15-by-9 forward V matrix, and write M=A B. Requiring all nine signed coordinate vectors to occur as rows of A means that each row of B equals a signed row of M. Signs and row order do not affect the lower model. It therefore suffices to inspect all nine-element subsets of the fifteen signed-distinct rows of M and retain those that are independent.

The source has six coordinate-unit rows and nine nonunit rows. A candidate basis containing t nonunit rows needs at least t gates just to produce those nonunit outputs from ordinary coordinates. Thus t>5 already excludes a five-gate conversion. Rank is computed modulo1009. This is exact for these integer9-by-9 matrices: every row has squared Euclidean norm at most3, so Hadamard bounds the determinant squared by3^9<1009^2. A nonzero integer determinant cannot disappear modulo1009.

For a remaining full-rank basis, include all nine ordinary input coordinate forms and its t nonunit requested output forms in the mandatory set T. A five-gate circuit has at most14 distinct signed wires in total. It can therefore introduce at most5-t helper forms. Full rank forces t>=3 for this source, leaving only helper budgets zero, one and two.

With zero helpers, exact signed-pair closure from the nine input forms decides feasibility. With one helper, select an actual gate equation with a nonzero helper coefficient. That coefficient is ±1 or ±2, so the helper is a signed target-pair sum/difference or half a target. The verifier enumerates the entire finite set and checks closure.

With two helpers, the standard formal-wire argument gives two actual gate equations whose helper coefficient matrix is invertible. The possible coefficient rows, up to sign, are e_i,2e_i,e_i±e_j,2e_i±e_j. Their RHS sets are, respectively, signed target-pair sums/differences, signed targets, signed targets and zero. Quotienting row/helper signs and permutations yields twelve active coefficient classes. For every RHS assignment the exact rational helper pair is A^{-1}R. Hence no denominator, coordinate-height or magnitude cutoff is used. Helpers coinciding with mandatory forms, each other up to sign, or zero are redundant and omitted.

The native verifier first applies the necessary formal-relation quotient-dimension test. For each survivor the Python verifier computes complete signed-pair closure from the nine ordinary input forms in T together with the proposed helpers. Reaching all requested output forms is sufficient for a circuit with at most five additions: at most five distinct noninput forms are available. It is also necessary for any circuit restricted to those wires. Cases with fewer helpers can be padded to the stated budget using unused target-pair sums, one gate per new helper; the nine unit roots alone provide72 distinct signed two-coordinate sums/differences, versus at most14 mandatory wires. At least two unused choices therefore exist.

All 464 relevant full-rank bases fail. The supplied V conversion uses six gates and its outputs form an independent target-row basis, proving minimum six.

For U, only four signed coordinate-unit directions occur among the target rows, while a full canonical basis needs six rows. At least two basis outputs are nonunit, requiring at least two additions. For the W-forward factor the same argument applies. Its actual output conversion is the transpose of that square invertible basis matrix. The linear-circuit transposition principle preserves additive cost for square full-rank maps, so its output conversion also needs at least two. The retained boundary costs2+6+2 attain the total lower bound10.

This argument assumes fixed numeric target coefficients up to signs, permits free sign/copy operations in the lower model, and counts conversions as separate stages. It does not constrain fused ordinary-coordinate circuits, unrestricted noncanonical bases, arbitrary projective term gauges, or paid scalar gates.
