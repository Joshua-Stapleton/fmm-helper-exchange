### A separate variant using coordinated product signs

For the supplied 7x7x8 maps, the verified circuits use **288 + 340 + 572 = 1200** literal additions. Coordinating the signs of multiplication terms across the three factors gives a separate ordinary-basis algorithm using **273 + 334 + 572 = 1179 additions**, plus the same **279 products**. All 153,664 tensor coefficients replay exactly. Its individual factor matrices differ from the uploaded maps by paired signs; it is not substituted into the fixed-map table above.

A term can change from `w * u(A) * v(B)` to `(-w) * (-u(A)) * v(B)` without changing its contribution. The joint orientation folds these signs into literal addition/subtraction circuits. Original matrix entries are still the inputs and outputs, so there is no basis conversion. The bundle independently checks both the sign relation and the full tensor. This uses the existing [joint orientation method](../methods/linear/orient.py); it is not a new general minimization algorithm.

For the exact uploaded 7x7x8 U and V maps, signed-wire search counts 273 and 334 are **not** complete literal costs. The strict programs pay another 15 and 6 additions respectively, including one shared paid zero in U. [The reusable repair](../methods/linear/reconstruction/paid_sign_repair.py) chooses wire orientations, explicitly pays remaining sign corrections and prunes unused gates. Its bounded orientation objective is not a proof of minimum circuit size. The standalone certificate includes uncorrected candidates, correction counts, seeds, timing and reproduction instructions.

The portfolio uses CSE, vector covering, 50% and 90% retained starts, dependency cuts, shared residual forests, weighted helper initialization and global finite-pool reconnection. Every W also receives a separately pooled transpose search; all reverse accumulations are charged before the original signed W map is checked. Upstream donor and completion reducers are Perminov's; the pool reconnection and orchestration are separate wrappers. This batch does not establish a generally superior SLP minimizer.

Each upstream solver uses one thread; three batch workers ran concurrently. Donor methods have 4-second stage limits, individual completion calls 1-second limits, and up to 12 attempts per reconstruction method. Pool searches and the extra W searches each have 8-second budgets; fixed-sign orientation has a 5-second solver budget. Methods can complete early. Each weighted stage requests two additional 1.5-second completions.

Observed per-matrix elapsed times, split into donor construction, pool assembly, search, orientation and extra W search, are in [counts.csv](../certificates/hl_tests_20261016/counts.csv) and [provenance](../certificates/hl_tests_20261016/provenance/). They include the current search from retained incumbents, not the historical time needed to discover those incumbents. Concurrent map times do not sum to campaign wall time. Installation, a stale-adapter rebuild and discarded cold-start attempts are excluded from map timings and recorded in claims.

Of 399 method-wrapper invocations, recorded statuses are COMPLETE: 285, PASS: 51, TIMEOUT: 63. A completed wrapper can contain unsuccessful or timed-out inner completion calls. Only exact completed circuits are admitted as donors; incomplete calls do not prove impossibility.

No basis change, unpaid unary negations, nonunit scalar operations or unused intermediate gates occur in the final literal programs. A/B entries use row-major order and C uses column-major order. This storage convention is not a basis transformation. No global optimality, new rank, exponent or measured hardware speedup is claimed.

Source decompositions and original circuits: Andrey Perminov, [FastMatrixMultiplication](https://github.com/dronperminov/FastMatrixMultiplication/tree/6c75fd36564b177165d6968efd4c9e6199266191). Every source circuit expands exactly to the uploaded map; all complete multiplication tensors are verified.
